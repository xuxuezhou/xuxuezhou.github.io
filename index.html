Research Interests

My current research focuses on world models in robotics: helping robots learn how the world works, so they can predict, plan and act more intelligently.

I enjoy mathematics-driven solutions. When tackling a real-world problem, I start by modeling simple cases, then build a more general theoretical framework to solve it, and finally refine that framework until it is elegant and intuitive.

My long-term interest is to study the brain 🧠. I want to understand the fundamental logic of cognition from the perspectives of cognitive science, psychology and biology, and use those insights to build the next generation of robot brains.

Selected Publications

  1. In Submission
    Vector-Quantized World Model: Latent Feedback Motion Planning for Robot Manipulation
    Xuezhou Xu*, Chung-Pang Wang*, Zhenzhen Li, and Benjamin Riviere
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2610.25600, 2026
    Project Page Abstract arXiv

    Visual world models have shown strong potential for robot planning, yet long-horizon manipulation remains challenging: prediction errors compound over autoregressive rollouts, search complexity grows with the horizon, and planners propose latent targets that the low-level controller cannot reliably reach. We introduce VQ-WM, a latent feedback motion planning framework that vector-quantizes the latent space by controller reachability, measured as first-passage time, so a discrete planner can search far ahead over reachable states while a continuous controller tracks each planned state with visual feedback.

  2. In Submission
    Toss2Reorient: Dynamic In-Air Object Reorientation via Toss-and-Catch with Dexterous Hand
    Xuezhou Xu*, Yutong Li*, Jinpeng Liu, and Xingyu Liu
    2026
    Project Page Abstract

    In-hand object reorientation has been extensively studied in robotic manipulation, yet it remains limited by slow execution and a tendency to become trapped in contact-constrained local optima when handling large or geometrically complex objects. These limitations often lead to prolonged manipulation time or failure on challenging shapes. We propose Toss2Reorient, a dynamic manipulation framework for in-air object reorientation through a toss-and-catch paradigm. By leveraging toss-and-catch motion together with coordinated hand–arm control, the method reduces the average execution time by 50.6% across 30 diverse objects in simulation. While dynamic manipulation introduces additional instability, wrist-mounted 6-axis force/torque (F/T) sensing helps stabilize interaction dynamics, with only a modest 1.8-point increase in macro-average drop rate relative to in-hand reorientation. The policy is trained using asymmetric Proximal Policy Optimization, where the critic has access to privileged object state information while the actor operates on partial observations. We evaluate the proposed framework on 30 diverse objects in simulation. Experimental results show substantially faster reorientation compared to conventional in-hand reorientation, particularly for large and geometrically complex objects. Furthermore, the learned policy demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization, successfully performing reorientation on unseen objects with different shapes and physical properties without additional training.
